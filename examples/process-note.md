# Usage note: “A note on how these essays were written”

## Starting point

After the first two essays, the writer wanted to acknowledge that their stories and ideas were their own while AI had written substantial prose. They were proud of publishing and concerned about whether the process provided enough practice in writing and speaking clearly. They began in Think mode, requested one question at a time, and initially wanted to compose the note themselves.

## What happened

- **Questions elicited useful material.** The writer described the authorship concern, the value of finally publishing, and the importance of organizing thoughts through dictation. They later explained how pressure to produce knowledge at work made continued use of AI important to them.
- **Working through excerpts became an obstacle.** After approving a structure, the writer struggled to visualize the piece through isolated passages and section-by-section choices. They explicitly requested questions followed by AI assembly and rewriting of their dictated answers into a connected draft.
- **The connected draft exposed a substantive omission.** The first draft underrepresented the broader reason for using AI and ended too quickly. Further dictation developed the need to remain productive while continuing to learn and understand. The writer directed changes to the framing, ending, repetition, and flow.
- **The goal remained open.** The writer questioned whether composing every word was the right target. They wanted more language to come from their own dictation over time, alongside clearer thinking and communication.

## Result and contributions

The note was [published on October 28, 2026](https://qvia.ai/writing/how-these-essays-were-written/).

The writer supplied the experiences, positions, substantial dictated material, and editorial decisions. AI proposed the structure, assembled and rewrote the continuous drafts, and supplied revised prose at the writer's request. Dictated source material and model-composed manuscript prose remained distinct contributions; no exact retention percentage was measured.

The writer proposed two signs of progress: explaining ideas without rereading the essay and dictating a coherent first draft. Neither was independently assessed in this session. Publication and approval of the result do not establish improved writing or speaking skill.

## Changes motivated by this use

Keep the author-first default and explicit permission for model-written prose. Offer a draft from dictation when requested, adapt a working format that creates administrative friction, and preserve the writer's central motivation through drafting and editing. Track the capability the writer wants to practice separately from the finished artifact.

This is a usage example from the same writer, not a new controlled experiment or evidence of effectiveness across writers.
