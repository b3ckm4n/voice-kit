# Second experiment: “I Needed Five Me’s. We Needed a New Way to Work.”

## Starting condition

The writer wanted to explore becoming an AI-native product manager, connecting personal experience to what companies need to support the transition. They began with a topic and background notes, then supplied specific experiences through dictation. They requested Think mode before architecture or drafting.

## Workflow

The work moved through Think, Architect, Draft, and Edit, with explicit agreement before architecture and drafting. Questions helped the writer articulate the central claim and supporting experiences. The AI proposed an architecture and produced a draft from that material. The writer recognized the voice and directed subsequent changes to the opening, structure, factual claims, and level of detail.

## What worked and what needed correction

- **Specific questions elicited substance.** The writer developed the distinction between producing work quickly and understanding enough to own it. They also explained how company support could free attention for product work.
- **Questions sometimes imposed the wrong pattern.** Repeated requests for a customer-feedback turning point did not fit an example whose meaning, for the writer, was ownership and learning through building. The useful response was to reflect that emphasis and change direction.
- **Drafting overstated a claim.** Discovered process options were initially described as implemented improvements. The writer corrected this: the approach was still undecided and unimplemented. Fluent synthesis had changed the status of the evidence.
- **Revisions needed a consistent current version.** Many excerpts and full drafts accumulated. Approved corrections, terminology, and removals needed to carry through later consolidations.

These observations motivate the compact working record in [the shared instructions](../VOICE-KIT.md) and adaptive questioning in [Think mode](../modes/think.md). The record should cover consequential claims and accepted decisions without becoming another administrative task.

## Result and authorship

The essay was [published on October 23, 2026](https://qvia.ai/writing/i-needed-five-mes/).

The writer supplied the experiences, central argument, and substantial language through dictation, corrected material facts, and chose the final framing. Under the earlier workflow, the writer explicitly requested model drafting. AI generated the first continuous draft and most subsequent prose revisions, proposed the architecture, and supplied the selected title and subtitle. No precise percentage of model-written language was measured.

The writer initially recognized the voice. While reviewing this report, they raised two further concerns: whether they had practiced composing enough and whether too much of the published expression came from the model. Recognizing the voice and owning the argument had not settled those questions.

Together with the first essay, this shows repeat use of an AI-assisted writing workflow by one writer. It does not demonstrate improvement in independent composition. There was no controlled comparison, independent assessment of understanding, quantified writing-time measure, or complete audit of prose provenance across both essays. Effectiveness across writers and models remains untested.

## Change for subsequent use

The writer chose author-first drafting as the new default: they write or dictate the manuscript, while AI helps through questions, diagnosis, and editing guidance. Model-written prose requires an explicit request. The shared instructions and Draft/Edit modes now distinguish composition practice from recognizing and approving the model's expression.

This is a change motivated by the experiment's limits, not an approach validated by the two published essays. The report records the result and the concern without claiming the new default has already improved writing skill.
