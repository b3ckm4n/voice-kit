# First experiment: “How I Became a Meat Proxy”

## Starting condition

The writer dictated ideas about AI and writing, then used AI to turn them into a nearly complete essay. The draft was fluent and broadly accurate, but trying to finish it produced writer's block. The prose had matured faster than the writer's understanding and ownership of the piece.

## Workflow change

The AI stopped trying to improve the draft and acted as a developmental editor instead:

1. It read the existing material and asked questions in comments.
2. The writer answered by dictating rather than line editing.
3. The answers exposed new ideas and more specific experiences.
4. The AI asked fewer, deeper follow-up questions.
5. Once the substance was mature, the work moved through architecture, drafting, and editing.

This led to the idea of **generative friction**: the interaction was useful when it caused the writer to produce more substance, not when it merely produced more polished text.

## Result

The process produced the first published essay: [“How I Became a Meat Proxy”](https://qvia.ai/writing/how-i-became-a-meat-proxy/).

That is evidence that the workflow helped one writer finish and publish one substantial piece in their own voice. It does not establish that the method works across writers, models, or writing tasks.

## Open questions

- Does the workflow improve understanding across repeated tasks?
- Which developmental questions reliably create new thinking?
- When does useful friction become administrative overhead?
- Can a voice profile preserve individuality without turning voice into a caricature?
- Where is the practical boundary between AI helping express a thought and the writer approving the AI's thought?
